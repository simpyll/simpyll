<h2>{"Starting Over Without Starting From Zero"}</h2>
<p><em>{"On learning something new after spending years getting good at something else."}</em></p>
<p>{"There’s a strange feeling that comes with being a beginner again."}</p>
<p>{"You spend years learning a profession. You develop skills, build confidence, make mistakes, and gradually reach a point where things that once seemed complicated become second nature."}</p>
<p>{"Then one day, you decide to learn something completely different."}</p>
<p>{"And suddenly, you're Googling things that everyone else seems to already know."}</p>
<p>{"That's more or less where I find myself."}</p>
<p>{"I've spent over a decade working as a registered nurse, primarily in critical care. I've worked in intensive care units, emergency departments, and just about every type of hospital unit in between."}</p>
<p>{"I'm comfortable in environments where things can change quickly. I know how to recognize when something isn't right, how to prioritize competing problems, and how to make decisions when the information is incomplete."}</p>
<p>{"But put me in front of a terminal with a JavaScript error?"}</p>
<p>{"I'm a beginner."}</p>
<p>{"And I've discovered that being a beginner again is both incredibly frustrating and surprisingly rewarding."}</p>
<h3>{"The Comfort of Knowing What You're Doing"}</h3>
<p>{"One of the things nobody really tells you about becoming experienced in a profession is how much of your knowledge eventually becomes invisible."}</p>
<p>{"When I first started nursing, everything required conscious effort."}</p>
<p>{"Interpreting laboratory results. Understanding medications. Recognizing changes in a patient's condition. Communicating with physicians. Managing multiple priorities simultaneously."}</p>
<p>{"There was a lot to learn, and it wasn't always obvious how the pieces fit together."}</p>
<p>{"Over time, things changed."}</p>
<p>{"I stopped thinking about every individual step and started recognizing patterns."}</p>
<p>{"A patient's blood pressure, heart rate, urine output, and laboratory results weren't just separate numbers anymore. Together, they told a story."}</p>
<p>{"I could walk into a room and notice things that I might not have recognized years earlier."}</p>
<p>{"That's what experience does."}</p>
<p>{"It turns deliberate effort into intuition."}</p>
<p>{"Of course, intuition isn't magic. It's accumulated knowledge, repeated exposure, mistakes, feedback, and thousands of small observations working together."}</p>
<p>{"But after enough time, you forget what it felt like not to know."}</p>
<p>{"Until you start something new."}</p>
<h3>{"Back to Square One"}</h3>
<p>{"My interest in software development started with a fairly simple realization."}</p>
<p>{"I use technology constantly, but I wanted to understand how it actually works."}</p>
<p>{"Not just how to operate software, but how to build it."}</p>
<p>{"How does a website turn a collection of files into something interactive? What happens when you click a button? How does information move between systems? Why does a small change in one place occasionally break something completely unrelated?"}</p>
<p>{"I wanted to understand the machinery behind the interface."}</p>
<p>{"So I started learning."}</p>
<p>{"And almost immediately, I ran into the uncomfortable reality of being inexperienced."}</p>
<p>{"There were unfamiliar terms everywhere."}</p>
<p>{"React. TypeScript. Node.js. Dependencies. Routing. Hydration. Build pipelines. Git."}</p>
<p>{"Even the explanations seemed to assume I already understood three other concepts."}</p>
<p>{"I'd solve one problem and discover two more."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes I'd spend an unreasonable amount of time trying to understand why something wasn't working, only to discover that I'd made a remarkably simple mistake."}</p>
<p>{"It's a humbling experience."}</p>
<p>{"Especially when you're accustomed to being competent."}</p>
<p>{"But something interesting happened as I kept going."}</p>
<p>{"I started recognizing familiar patterns."}</p>
<p>{"Not necessarily in the code itself, but in the way the problems worked."}</p>
<h3>{"Different Fields, Familiar Problems"}</h3>
<p>{"At first glance, critical care nursing and software development don't have much in common."}</p>
<p>{"One involves patients, medications, physiology, and clinical judgment."}</p>
<p>{"The other involves code, applications, data, and computer systems."}</p>
<p>{"But underneath the surface, both require a similar way of thinking."}</p>
<p>{"You encounter a problem."}</p>
<p>{"You collect information."}</p>
<p>{"You identify possible explanations."}</p>
<p>{"You test assumptions."}</p>
<p>{"You make a change."}</p>
<p>{"You observe what happens."}</p>
<p>{"Then you adjust."}</p>
<p>{"In healthcare, we call parts of that process clinical reasoning."}</p>
<p>{"In software development, we might call it debugging."}</p>
<p>{"The stakes are obviously different. A broken website isn't the same thing as a deteriorating patient."}</p>
<p>{"But the underlying habit of approaching uncertainty systematically is surprisingly transferable."}</p>
<p>{"When something doesn't work, my instinct is to ask questions."}</p>
<p>{"What's actually happening?"}</p>
<p>{"What should be happening?"}</p>
<p>{"What changed?"}</p>
<p>{"What evidence supports my explanation?"}</p>
<p>{"What am I assuming without realizing it?"}</p>
<p>{"Those questions are useful almost everywhere."}</p>
<p>{"And they're a reminder that while I might be starting from the beginning in software development, I'm not starting from nothing."}</p>
<h3>{"The Difference Between Knowledge and Thinking"}</h3>
<p>{"I've been thinking about this distinction quite a bit."}</p>
<p>{"When we talk about learning something new, we often focus on acquiring information."}</p>
<p>{"Learn the syntax. Memorize the commands. Understand the framework. Practice the techniques."}</p>
<p>{"And those things matter."}</p>
<p>{"But knowledge is only part of expertise."}</p>
<p>{"There's also the ability to work through unfamiliar situations."}</p>
<p>{"To recognize patterns."}</p>
<p>{"To distinguish important information from distractions."}</p>
<p>{"To tolerate uncertainty without immediately jumping to conclusions."}</p>
<p>{"To keep working when the answer isn't obvious."}</p>
<p>{"Those abilities don't disappear when you change disciplines."}</p>
<p>{"They just need to be applied in a different context."}</p>
<p>{"I don't know nearly as much about software engineering as someone who has spent ten years building applications."}</p>
<p>{"But I do know how to learn complicated systems."}</p>
<p>{"I know what it's like to encounter something overwhelming, break it into smaller pieces, and gradually make sense of it."}</p>
<p>{"I've done that before."}</p>
<p>{"Just with different tools."}</p>
<h3>{"Building Something You Don't Fully Understand"}</h3>
<p>{"One of my first substantial projects has been this website."}</p>
<p>{"Simpyll."}</p>
<p>{"I wanted somewhere to experiment with technology, document what I'm learning, and build things that interested me."}</p>
<p>{"I could have used an existing website builder."}</p>
<p>{"That would have been faster."}</p>
<p>{"It probably would have been easier."}</p>
<p>{"And I almost certainly would have avoided a considerable number of headaches."}</p>
<p>{"Instead, I decided to build it using React, TypeScript, and a collection of tools I was still learning to understand."}</p>
<p>{"Was that the most efficient approach?"}</p>
<p>{"Probably not."}</p>
<p>{"But efficiency wasn't really the point."}</p>
<p>{"The point was to learn how the pieces fit together."}</p>
<p>{"I've spent time working through routing, styling, deployment, content publishing, animations, accessibility, and problems I didn't even know existed when I started."}</p>
<p>{"I've also used AI extensively throughout the process."}</p>
<p>{"That introduces an interesting challenge of its own."}</p>
<p>{"It's now possible to build things much faster than you can fully understand them."}</p>
<p>{"An AI assistant can generate code, explain errors, suggest architectures, and implement features that might otherwise take a beginner days to figure out."}</p>
<p>{"That's an extraordinary advantage."}</p>
<p>{"But it also creates a temptation to confuse a working result with actual understanding."}</p>
<p>{"Those aren't the same thing."}</p>
<p>{"A website can function perfectly while the person building it has only a vague idea of why."}</p>
<p>{"I'm trying to remain aware of that distinction."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes the right approach is to use the tools available and keep moving."}</p>
<p>{"Other times, it's worth slowing down, reading the code, asking questions, and understanding what actually happened."}</p>
<p>{"I'm still figuring out that balance."}</p>
<p>{"And I suspect I'll be figuring it out for a long time."}</p>
<h3>{"The Frustration of Being Bad at Something"}</h3>
<p>{"There's an uncomfortable part of learning that I think becomes more noticeable as you get older."}</p>
<p>{"You know what competence feels like."}</p>
<p>{"You know what good work looks like."}</p>
<p>{"But you can't consistently produce it yet."}</p>
<p>{"You can recognize an elegant website without knowing how to build one."}</p>
<p>{"You can appreciate well-designed software without understanding the architectural decisions behind it."}</p>
<p>{"You can see the destination long before you understand the route."}</p>
<p>{"That gap can be frustrating."}</p>
<p>{"It creates the feeling that you should be progressing faster."}</p>
<p>{"That you should understand more."}</p>
<p>{"That something obvious must be escaping you."}</p>
<p>{"And sometimes it is."}</p>
<p>{"But I think part of the problem is that we compare our beginner abilities in one discipline with our experienced abilities in another."}</p>
<p>{"That's not a particularly fair comparison."}</p>
<p>{"It took years to develop confidence in nursing."}</p>
<p>{"There were countless shifts, unfamiliar situations, mistakes, difficult conversations, and lessons along the way."}</p>
<p>{"Why would I expect to become proficient in software development after a few weeks?"}</p>
<p>{"I wouldn't expect a new nurse to understand everything about critical care immediately."}</p>
<p>{"Yet it's surprisingly easy to hold yourself to that kind of standard when you're the beginner."}</p>
<h3>{"AI Changes the Learning Curve, but Not the Destination"}</h3>
<p>{"One of the things that makes learning software development particularly interesting right now is the role of artificial intelligence."}</p>
<p>{"We're entering a period where the barriers to building software are changing dramatically."}</p>
<p>{"You no longer need to know every detail of a programming language to create something useful."}</p>
<p>{"You can describe what you want, collaborate with an AI system, and produce working applications much earlier in the learning process."}</p>
<p>{"That opens doors for people who might never have considered building software before."}</p>
<p>{"People with expertise in healthcare, education, engineering, finance, or dozens of other fields can start turning their ideas into functional tools."}</p>
<p>{"I find that genuinely exciting."}</p>
<p>{"But I don't think it eliminates the value of understanding what you're building."}</p>
<p>{"If anything, it makes judgment more important."}</p>
<p>{"You still need to know what problem you're trying to solve."}</p>
<p>{"You need to recognize when the proposed solution doesn't make sense."}</p>
<p>{"You need to ask useful questions."}</p>
<p>{"You need to evaluate tradeoffs."}</p>
<p>{"And you need enough understanding to recognize when something that appears to work is actually broken."}</p>
<p>{"AI can accelerate the process."}</p>
<p>{"It can't automatically give you the judgment that comes from experience."}</p>
<p>{"That still has to be developed."}</p>
<h3>{"Maybe Being a Beginner Is an Advantage"}</h3>
<p>{"There's another side to starting over that I didn't initially appreciate."}</p>
<p>{"When you're new to something, you haven't yet learned all the reasons why things supposedly can't be done."}</p>
<p>{"You ask basic questions."}</p>
<p>{"You challenge assumptions without realizing they're assumptions."}</p>
<p>{"You approach familiar problems from unfamiliar directions."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes that leads nowhere."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes you discover that the conventional approach exists for a very good reason."}</p>
<p>{"But occasionally, a beginner notices something that an experienced person has stopped questioning."}</p>
<p>{"I see a version of this in healthcare."}</p>
<p>{"People entering a profession sometimes ask why a particular process works the way it does."}</p>
<p>{"The answer might be grounded in evidence, safety, or years of practical experience."}</p>
<p>{"But sometimes the answer is simply that we've always done it that way."}</p>
<p>{"Being inexperienced doesn't automatically make your perspective better."}</p>
<p>{"It does, however, make it different."}</p>
<p>{"And different perspectives can be useful."}</p>
<p>{"I don't want to lose that curiosity as I learn more."}</p>
<p>{"I want to understand the established approaches without becoming so attached to them that I stop asking questions."}</p>
<h3>{"What I'm Actually Trying to Build"}</h3>
<p>{"At some point, this stopped being just about learning to code."}</p>
<p>{"I started thinking more about the kinds of things I could create with the knowledge."}</p>
<p>{"Tools that make complicated information easier to understand."}</p>
<p>{"Interactive explanations of difficult concepts."}</p>
<p>{"Better ways to organize information."}</p>
<p>{"Experiments that connect healthcare, technology, and systems thinking."}</p>
<p>{"Small projects that might be useful to someone else."}</p>
<p>{"And sometimes things that exist simply because they're interesting."}</p>
<p>{"That's what I want Simpyll to become."}</p>
<p>{"Not a polished monument to expertise I haven't earned."}</p>
<p>{"Not a collection of tutorials pretending I know more than I do."}</p>
<p>{"Just a place to build, experiment, document, and improve."}</p>
<p>{"Some projects will work."}</p>
<p>{"Some probably won't."}</p>
<p>{"Some ideas will turn out to be less interesting than I expected."}</p>
<p>{"But each one will teach me something."}</p>
<p>{"And over time, those lessons will accumulate."}</p>
<p>{"That's how expertise develops."}</p>
<p>{"Not all at once, but through repeated attempts to understand things that initially seem confusing."}</p>
<h3>{"Starting Over Isn't Really Starting Over"}</h3>
<p>{"I've come to think that the phrase "}<em>{"starting over"}</em>{" is a little misleading."}</p>
<p>{"It suggests that everything before the new beginning has been erased."}</p>
<p>{"That you're back at the bottom."}</p>
<p>{"That your previous experience somehow stops counting."}</p>
<p>{"But that's not how learning works."}</p>
<p>{"We carry our experiences into whatever comes next."}</p>
<p>{"The knowledge might not transfer directly, but the habits often do."}</p>
<p>{"The patience."}</p>
<p>{"The problem-solving."}</p>
<p>{"The judgment."}</p>
<p>{"The willingness to ask questions."}</p>
<p>{"The ability to recognize when you don't know enough."}</p>
<p>{"Those things matter."}</p>
<p>{"I might be a beginner when it comes to building software, but I'm not a beginner at learning difficult things."}</p>
<p>{"And that changes how I look at the process."}</p>
<p>{"I'm not trying to abandon one identity and replace it with another."}</p>
<p>{"I'm trying to add something new."}</p>
<p>{"A decade from now, I have no idea what I'll be building, what technologies I'll be using, or how much of today's software development landscape will still look familiar."}</p>
<p>{"But I hope I'm still learning things that make me uncomfortable."}</p>
<p>{"Still asking questions."}</p>
<p>{"Still building things I don't entirely know how to build yet."}</p>
<p>{"Because the more I think about it, the more I realize that being a beginner isn't something you necessarily need to escape."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes it's something worth choosing."}</p>
