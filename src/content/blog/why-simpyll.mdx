<p><em>{"On Jekyll, Unix, and the idea that complexity is best managed through simplicity."}</em></p>
<p>{"Every project needs a name."}</p>
<p>{"Some names are carefully engineered. Others emerge from a late-night idea, a passing reference, or a word that just sounds right."}</p>
<p>{"Simpyll started somewhere in between."}</p>
<p>{"The original inspiration was pretty straightforward: "}<strong>{"simple + Jekyll"}</strong>{"."}</p>
<p>{"Jekyll, the static site generator closely associated with GitHub Pages, represented something I appreciated about the web. You could write content, generate a website, and publish it without maintaining a complicated backend or an elaborate infrastructure."}</p>
<p>{"There was something appealing about that."}</p>
<p>{"Take a collection of files, apply a little structure, and turn them into something useful."}</p>
<p>{"I liked the simplicity. I liked the connection to GitHub. And I liked the idea of building a personal corner of the internet without needing an entire platform behind it."}</p>
<p>{"Somewhere along the way, "}<em>{"simple"}</em>{" and "}<em>{"Jekyll"}</em>{" became "}<strong>{"Simpyll"}</strong>{"."}</p>
<p>{"It was a little unusual, easy to remember, and just nerdy enough to feel appropriate."}</p>
<p>{"But the more I thought about what I wanted to build, the more the name started to mean something else."}</p>
<p>{"Something bigger than a static website."}</p>
<h3>{"The Unix Idea"}</h3>
<p>{"I've always been drawn to the philosophy behind Unix and Linux."}</p>
<p>{"Not necessarily because they're simple systems. Anyone who has spent enough time configuring Linux knows that simplicity is not always the first word that comes to mind."}</p>
<p>{"What interests me is the philosophy underneath them."}</p>
<p>{"In the early development of Unix, a particular approach to software design emerged: instead of building one enormous program responsible for everything, build smaller programs that each perform a specific task."}</p>
<p>{"Make those programs reliable."}</p>
<p>{"Give them consistent ways to communicate."}</p>
<p>{"Then combine them to solve larger problems."}</p>
<p>{"Doug McIlroy, one of the early architects of Unix, helped articulate this philosophy:"}</p>
<blockquote><p>{"Make each program do one thing well."}</p></blockquote>
<p>{"That principle sounds almost too obvious to be interesting."}</p>
<p>{"Of course software should do its job well."}</p>
<p>{"But the important part isn't just doing one thing. It's what becomes possible when independent pieces are designed to work together."}</p>
<p>{"A small utility might not be particularly impressive on its own. Neither is a pipe connecting two commands."}</p>
<p>{"But connect enough useful tools, and suddenly you can accomplish something surprisingly sophisticated."}</p>
<p>{"The complexity doesn't disappear."}</p>
<p><strong>{"It becomes manageable through composition."}</strong></p>
<p>{"That's the part that has always stuck with me."}</p>
<h3>{"Simplicity Is Not the Absence of Complexity"}</h3>
<p>{"There's an important distinction between something being simple and something being simplistic."}</p>
<p>{"A simplistic solution ignores complexity."}</p>
<p>{"A simple solution understands complexity well enough to organize it."}</p>
<p>{"Those are very different things."}</p>
<p>{"A well-designed system can accomplish extraordinarily complicated tasks while remaining understandable at the level of its individual components."}</p>
<p>{"Think about a command-line pipeline:"}</p>
<p><code>{"cat access.log | grep \"ERROR\" | sort | uniq -c"}</code></p>
<p>{"Each utility has a relatively narrow responsibility."}</p>
<p>{"One reads data. Another filters it. Another sorts it. Another counts repeated lines."}</p>
<p>{"Together, they create something more useful than any one component could accomplish alone."}</p>
<p>{"You don't need to rewrite the filtering utility every time you want to analyze a different file."}</p>
<p>{"You don't need a specialized application for every combination of operations."}</p>
<p>{"You assemble the tools you already have."}</p>
<p>{"And when your needs change, you change the composition."}</p>
<p>{"This is an incredibly powerful idea."}</p>
<p>{"It also suggests something that applies far beyond software:"}</p>
<p><strong>{"The best way to manage complexity isn't always to eliminate it. Sometimes it's to give it better boundaries."}</strong></p>
<h3>{"Why This Matters to Simpyll"}</h3>
<p>{"When I started building Simpyll, I knew I wanted a personal website."}</p>
<p>{"A place to write, share projects, document experiments, and explore ideas."}</p>
<p>{"But I didn't want to create a complicated system just for the sake of having one."}</p>
<p>{"I wanted something that could grow organically."}</p>
<p>{"A blog shouldn't need to know how a project portfolio works."}</p>
<p>{"A project page shouldn't need to understand how an article gets published."}</p>
<p>{"A publishing workflow shouldn't require me to manually rebuild the entire website every time I write something."}</p>
<p>{"Each piece should have a responsibility."}</p>
<p>{"Each piece should work independently where practical."}</p>
<p>{"And together, those pieces should form a cohesive experience."}</p>
<p>{"That's the ideal, anyway."}</p>
<p>{"The actual development process has involved plenty of experimentation, unexpected problems, and moments where something that seemed simple turned out to be anything but."}</p>
<p>{"I've encountered build failures, hydration issues, publishing problems, and the occasional feature that became much more complicated than I originally intended."}</p>
<p>{"But that's part of the process."}</p>
<p>{"Simplicity isn't something you achieve once and preserve forever."}</p>
<p>{"It's something you continually work toward."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes that means introducing a new abstraction."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes it means removing one."}</p>
<p>{"And sometimes it means admitting that the clever solution you spent hours building probably wasn't necessary in the first place."}</p>
<h3>{"Build Small. Connect Everything."}</h3>
<p>{"One of my favorite things about the Unix philosophy is that it doesn't demand you predict the future."}</p>
<p>{"In fact, it encourages almost the opposite."}</p>
<p>{"Build something useful now."}</p>
<p>{"Give it a clear purpose."}</p>
<p>{"Make it capable of working with other things."}</p>
<p>{"Then let future needs determine what comes next."}</p>
<p>{"This is how I want Simpyll to evolve."}</p>
<p>{"The website itself is one project, but it isn't the only one I want to explore."}</p>
<p>{"I'm interested in building software tools, medical calculators, healthcare education resources, databases, and experiments involving artificial intelligence."}</p>
<p>{"Some of those ideas might become substantial applications."}</p>
<p>{"Others might remain small utilities."}</p>
<p>{"A few might never make it beyond an experiment."}</p>
<p>{"That's fine."}</p>
<p>{"Not every idea needs to become a platform."}</p>
<p>{"Not every project needs to become a business."}</p>
<p>{"Not every experiment needs to justify its existence through some grand long-term vision."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes building something small, useful, and understandable is enough."}</p>
<p>{"And sometimes those small things become the foundation for something much larger."}</p>
<h3>{"The Temptation to Build Everything"}</h3>
<p>{"There's an interesting contradiction in software development."}</p>
<p>{"The more capable our tools become, the easier it is to build things we don't actually need."}</p>
<p>{"Modern frameworks, cloud platforms, APIs, and AI-assisted development make it possible to assemble sophisticated applications with remarkably little effort compared to the past."}</p>
<p>{"That's exciting."}</p>
<p>{"It's also dangerous in a very ordinary way."}</p>
<p>{"When adding another feature becomes easy, it's tempting to keep adding features."}</p>
<p>{"When building another abstraction takes only a few minutes, it's tempting to keep creating abstractions."}</p>
<p>{"Eventually, a small project can become an elaborate collection of systems that exist primarily to support one another."}</p>
<p>{"I've already felt that temptation while developing Simpyll."}</p>
<p>{"There's always another feature to implement, another optimization to make, another interesting technical problem to solve."}</p>
<p>{"And to be clear, I enjoy that part."}</p>
<p>{"Experimentation is one of the reasons the project exists."}</p>
<p>{"But I also want to develop the discipline to ask a more important question:"}</p>
<p><em>{"Does this actually make the system better?"}</em></p>
<p>{"Not more impressive."}</p>
<p>{"Not more complicated."}</p>
<p>{"Not more technically interesting."}</p>
<p>{"Better."}</p>
<p>{"Sometimes the answer will be yes."}</p>
<p>{"Other times, the best feature is the one I decide not to build."}</p>
<h3>{"A Philosophy Beyond Software"}</h3>
<p>{"What I appreciate most about this way of thinking is how easily it extends beyond programming."}</p>
<p>{"In healthcare, complicated systems depend on people, processes, and technologies performing distinct responsibilities while communicating effectively."}</p>
<p>{"In education, difficult subjects become more approachable when broken into concepts that can be understood individually and connected progressively."}</p>
<p>{"Even learning to program follows a similar pattern."}</p>
<p>{"You don't need to understand every layer of a modern application before you can build something useful."}</p>
<p>{"You learn one concept."}</p>
<p>{"Then another."}</p>
<p>{"You connect them."}</p>
<p>{"Eventually, things that once seemed impossibly complicated begin to make sense."}</p>
<p>{"That's not because the complexity vanished."}</p>
<p>{"It's because you've developed a way to organize it."}</p>
<p>{"I find that idea reassuring, particularly as someone approaching software development from a background outside traditional computer science."}</p>
<p>{"I don't need to know everything before I start."}</p>
<p>{"I need to understand the next piece well enough to build it."}</p>
<p>{"Then I can move on to the next."}</p>
<h3>{"A Name That Became a Direction"}</h3>
<p>{"So why Simpyll?"}</p>
<p>{"Originally, it was a play on Jekyll and the word "}<em>{"simple"}</em>{"."}</p>
<p>{"That's still part of the story."}</p>
<p>{"But today, I think the name represents something more deliberate."}</p>
<p>{"It's a reminder to favor clarity over cleverness."}</p>
<p>{"To build components that serve a purpose."}</p>
<p>{"To create systems that can grow without becoming impossible to understand."}</p>
<p>{"To experiment freely without assuming every experiment needs to become something enormous."}</p>
<p>{"And to remember that sophisticated things don't necessarily require sophisticated beginnings."}</p>
<p>{"I don't expect Simpyll to embody these principles perfectly."}</p>
<p>{"In fact, I'm almost certain I'll violate them repeatedly as I learn."}</p>
<p>{"I'll overengineer things."}</p>
<p>{"I'll build features I don't need."}</p>
<p>{"I'll occasionally make something complicated when a much simpler approach was available."}</p>
<p>{"Then, hopefully, I'll recognize it and make things better."}</p>
<p>{"That's part of learning."}</p>
<p>{"And that's part of what this website is meant to document."}</p>
<p>{"Simpyll isn't really about achieving some perfect state of minimalism."}</p>
<p>{"It's about pursuing simplicity while building things that matter to me."}</p>
<p>{"One idea."}</p>
<p>{"One component."}</p>
<p>{"One experiment at a time."}</p>
<p><strong>{"Keep the pieces simple. Let the possibilities become complex."}</strong></p>
<p>{"That's Simpyll."}</p>
